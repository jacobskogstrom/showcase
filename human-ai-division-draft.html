Man vs Machine: Who Does What

Not a theory. A pattern pulled from real work — every project Jacob has run with Claude over the past months. Same seven splits keep showing up. This is the raw material for a Bitesize / Telling Technology episode.

Working draft · not final
The question isn't "what can AI do." It's "what should stay with the human even when the AI could technically do it." Every project below drew that line somewhere. The line is always in the same seven places.
01

Decide vs. Build

Human

Makes the irreversible call. Build vs. buy. Sign vs. walk.

AI

Turns the call into a working system, same day.

Example: Family budget project — Jacob decided "build, not buy." Claude shipped the private Sheet, the CSV normalizer, and the Dashboard budget tab.
02

Relationship vs. Record

Human

Has the lunch. Makes the call. Shakes the hand.

AI

Turns the moment into structured, searchable memory.

Example: Contact-hub — Jacob says "lunch with X." Claude parses it, drafts updates across contacts.md, Google Contacts, the Networking calendar, and the Jobs sheet — applies only after Jacob confirms.
03

Taste vs. Iteration

Human

Says what's wrong the moment it's wrong. No negotiation.

AI

Iterates until it clears the bar — however many passes that takes.

Example: Jacob rejects clean-but-flat UI, wants Linear/Vercel/Apple-grade craft — Claude re-renders until it lands. Same reflex killed a whole episode topic outright: one veto, gone for good.
04

Strategy vs. Surveillance

Human

Sets the filter — what counts as a fit, what's worth watching.

AI

Watches continuously so the human doesn't have to.

Example: Job-hunt fit rules (skip commercial/agreements-PM, skip hardware-heavy EM) feed an always-on job board scan. Same shape runs the price monitor and the flight-deal hunter.
05

Authorization vs. Execution

Human

Owns risk. Anything irreversible or public needs a yes.

AI

Does everything up to that line, then stops and asks.

Example: CI pipeline gate — Claude diagnoses and prepares any fix, but never pushes to the main branch, even from a phone message, without Jacob's explicit go-ahead first.
06

Voice vs. Voice-matching

Human

Is the voice on anything that leaves the building.

AI

Drafts in that voice, never publishes it unseen.

Example: Standing rule — no recruiter email, LinkedIn post, or client message goes out in "Jacob's voice" without Jacob reading the draft first.
07

Correction vs. Institution

Human

Catches the miss. "You should've checked that file."

AI

Turns the one-time correction into a rule that never breaks again.

Example: A missed git worktree, a wrong contact-lookup order, a sheet write that broke mid-file — each became a standing rule after exactly one correction, not two.

The pattern

Human owns: judgment, relationships, taste, risk, and voice. Things that can't be undone, or that represent someone to another human.

AI owns: memory, drafting, monitoring, and mechanical follow-through. Things that are reversible, and that scale by being done more often, not more carefully.

The AIOS itself — this whole setup — is the case study. Every project in it drew this same line somewhere.
